Today is Sunday, the day of the sun. I wake before the house, as every morning deserves its own genesis.

Seven. Sometimes earlier. The exact minute is negotiable; the principle is sacred. I inherit the corridors — a pale sovereign of staircases and hallways, a domestic vampire permitted to haunt in peace. There is something indecent about being perceived too early in the day. The mind, at that hour, is amphibious. Half dream, half doctrine. Thoughts arrive barefoot. I let them roam unsupervised. It is dangerous to leash consciousness too quickly. It must stretch in the dark, test the perimeter, rehearse small insanities before settling.

I put on my beloved sheepskin slippers from Zakopane. Pillows aligned like loyal soldiers. Cover smoothed until it denies all evidence of the sleeper. The bed must not reveal what occurred upon it — doubt, ambition, fever, entire empires constructed and dismantled between midnight and four.

Downstairs, the house is skeletal and cold. I brush my teeth. Further down, into the kitchen, which feels slightly subterranean at this hour, I open the garden door. Winter air enters like a baptism, striking the lungs cleanly. I adore the cold with the loyalty others reserve for summer. Let them mourn their dreadful season of excess; frost only strengthens my spirit.

My cat slips inside, vast and white, carrying the scent of soil and bark.

I drink warm water. Traditional Chinese medicine says it awakens the organs gently, to stir internal fires without shock. Whether or not the theory is precise is beside the point. The body believes what it is told often enough.

I read. Currently, Kamien na kamieniu. I ought to read more in Polish; it would not feel so strenuous.

Eventually, I break the fast: kiwi, kefir, coffee. While I eat, I practise Russian, letting the sounds roll over my tongue, exact and strange. I enjoy speaking languages no one in the house can understand. It feels conspiratorial and mildly treasonous.

Around this time, the house begins to stir. Footsteps. Pipes. A cough from somewhere above. Even on a Sunday, life resumes its gentle insistence. Slowly, the spell begins to thin.

I retreat to the shower, carrying my speaker like contraband. Steam blooms. Music detonates against tile. I sing as though observed by an invisible jury. Water runs down my skin, supposedly washing away impurities — physical, spiritual. Whether it removes metaphysical residue remains unverified. Still — I emerge convinced of renewal.

I apply my Korean skincare with devotional seriousness, layer after layer, hydration as metaphysics. Then, I brush my long dark hair until it complies.

I carry the second coffee upstairs like an offering to myself and sit at my desk — my office. A candle, bought in Estonia, ignites. It feels hygge. Juniper threads its scent through the room, and my desk expands to the Cairngorms, frost-dark hills and moorlands.

Sundays are for wandering through my thoughts, unbound by obligation. Yet I cannot afford to do nothing — I must make something of myself. I open Pinterest with strategic seriousness. Research, I call it. Visual calibration. World-building. I collect images of exquisite interior designs, faces with excellent bone structure, blackberry-stained napkins, buttery silk pyjamas of a honeyed alabaster colour, niche tweets, archive runway dresses, mysterious dreamlike destinations, and braids. This is part of my work, I insist. Industrious, meticulous, bordering on genius, occasionally ridiculous. And yet, eventually, I must turn this studious curation into creation.

There comes a particular, almost violent, moment when one must close all tabs and face the blank page. I do this with the solemnity of a general planning an invasion. Spine straight. Jaw set. I am a graduate of a prestigious university. I am a mastermind.

Within minutes, I drift.

Yesterday there was a video of snow with text over it: "when the weather feels like silly caucasian girl likes to play with samurai swords." It felt profound for absolutely no reason. I saved it. I wish it were snowing.

Which reminds me — I watched Sniegu juz nigdy nie bedzie recently. I still cannot decide what I thought of it. But do I really have to know what I thought of every film I have seen?

And anyway, the Olympic women's free skating happened Thursday. Ice. Pirouettes. Jumps. Sparkles catching the arena light. Blades slicing crescents. Oh, and seeing people win makes me so happy — it fills my heart with sparkles.

This has nothing to do with what I am supposed to be writing.

Focus, Margot.

I like to wander on the verge of madness. Or at least to rehearse it. There is something flattering about imagining oneself slightly unhinged — a misunderstood savant, muttering truths the village is not yet ready to receive.

Stop saying shit like that. It is unprofessional. Just write. Just type something.

I speak aloud sometimes, in accents vaguely aristocratic, vaguely criminal. I conduct interviews with myself. I answer brilliantly. Hours pass in this oscillation between revelation and poppycock. I produce paragraphs of impressive density that, upon rereading, resemble decorative fog. I delete them with restrained tragedy. I try again. I become precise. I become, briefly, formidable. Before drifting again, deep into the abyss of my own mind, where every thought arrives wearing a costume.

At some point, I am summoned downstairs for lunch. My brother and grandparents are already seated. I check my cutlery, for my brother likes to give me the worst forks and knives, and I am very keen about my cutlery. They speak many absurdities and bits of nonsense that I find very amusing and nourishing: non sequiturs, observations about weather, neighbours, and soup. I listen with anthropological fascination. I nod gravely, temporarily embedded among mortals.

Afterwards, I ascend to my tower, detouring past my grandmother's bedroom, where my cat has installed himself like a minor deity. I bury my face in his vast white belly. He smells of warmth, earth, and mineral. I consider this medicinal.

Then comes the black hole of the Sunday afternoon. I try to get everything done by morning, because come midday, I like to spiral into this peculiar void. I drift between activities without obligation: reorganise, clean, correspondence, or bother my brother because affection must be exercised. I bombard him with arcane trivia. When he actually engages and asks for the source, I roll my eyes: Divine intuition, you silly goose! I preach to myself about discipline, about the importance of keeping dreams alive. I daydream, listen to music, write further absurdities, send acutely funny TikToks to my dear ones, and bask in boredom, which I believe is crucial.

This is the hour when my certainty thins. By now the rituals have worn off. The genius has gone quiet. The room is simply a room again.

Sometimes I suspect I am merely rearranging my own mediocrity. Strip away the ceremony and I am just a girl trying, as everyone does, to become something.

I have learnt not to trust my thoughts at this time of day — they grow small and mean. So instead, I open another tab and research something obscure and unnecessary. After all, knowledge is power, they say.

By four, it is l'heure du gouter. I peel an orange with ceremonial care. I drink chaga — dark as obsidian rain, tasting birch bark and smoke. A taiga tonic. Outside, winter conducts its slow erasure of colour. Trees stand skeletal; sky pale and undecided. While the earth freezes, I am consuming a sun. The thought pleases me disproportionately. I feel briefly omnipotent.

Before night fully descends, I go for a walk in the forest. Half-swallowed by moss and bramble lie old German bunkers, slowly reclaimed by roots and damp. Many years ago, a murder occurred in one. Passing them feels theatrical. Slightly dangerous. Very thrilling. Just beyond the forest, a farm stretches across fields dotted with stables and wooden fences, bathed in the molten glow of a sinking sun, light glinting off weathered wood. For a split second, I feel like a cowboy, surveying my domain.

In the evening, I conduct my final rites. I stretch with priestly seriousness, believing I am a prima ballerina preparing for tonight's show. Incense lit to cast out bad spirits — or at least to make the room smell like something is being exorcised. I apply skincare again, still Korean: toners, serums, balms — noni, centella asiatica, glacial algae. It makes me feel like a chemist, playing with mixtures and textures, like a child making mud soup, absorbed and pleased with the world.

Sunday evenings are made for watching films. Today I choose Paper Moon. I study it as if I, too, am running a minor con — a wandering trickster with charm and grit, surviving by guile and grace on cracked asphalt and borrowed hope. I loved it so much it may enter my very selective list of favourites.

I scroll a little, still pretending it is research. I drink warm water. Traditional Chinese medicine says that drinking it before sleep soothes the stomach and calms the body. I whisper prayers to the stars. Because, after all, wishes do come true.

And so, after being a morning spectre, a spiralling genius, an anthropologist, a dreamer, my own preacher, a cowboy, a prima ballerina, and a cinephile, I retire to sleep. I lie in the dark and review the day. Perhaps none of this unfolded with the significance I assigned it. Perhaps I merely woke, ate, wrote little, thought too much, and went to bed. Perhaps the orange was just an orange. Perhaps there was no murderer in the bunker.

But this is how it happened to me. And if reality is largely the story one can sustain about it, then I have lived today extensively. Tomorrow, I will wake before the house again.
