I hold onto things. I was born with eyes too wide open and never learned to close them.

There is something in me that cannot watch a moment pass without needing to trap it somewhere inside — deep, under the ribs, in the marrow, where time does not pry so easy.

Ever since I could, I have photographed everything. Filmed my friends laughing, my siblings dancing. I made little videos of our trips that felt important to keep. Drew landscapes from places I feared I would never see again. Recorded voices and sounds, knowing one day they would be gone.

I made my grandfather tell the same stories over and over until I knew them the way you know a psalm. And eventually, when all of that was not enough, I started writing. Not because my life is extraordinary, but because all of it is precious to me. And because nothing lasts — this is the whole problem and the whole beauty of it.

I hold it all. The sound of cuckoos in the morning, when the trees are just starting to green, and what spring does to the old oak. Laughing so hard my whole body forgets how to be sad. Cold lake water on a hot day, the good tired in my bones after. His scratched-up hands and deep black eyes. Crackle of firewood, smoke in my hair, sugar blistering on marshmallows. Braiding my friend's barley-blond hair. Petrichor. Sipping coffee and gossiping with my grandpa at his sister's house. Sunset on Wakatipu Lake. The road trip playlist we had memorised by Montana. My ma picking me up from school, her deep green eyes bright as pine. Learning to drive at thirteen on beat-up country roads. His jokes that make me blush real hard. The bite of a frozen, starlit night. Godawful karaoke with siblings. Doing what we know is bad and running home red-cheeked and grinning, hearts still hammering, full of criminal joy. The creek rolling cold from the mountains. The smell of drying mushrooms in the stairwell, the whole block smelling of earth and autumn. Renting horror movies with my pa from the old video club. Afternoons making cookies, with flour on our hands and music too loud. Grandma's garden. Reading on the front porch while everyone is still sleeping.

But also the times hope left without saying goodbye. Heartaches that dug a hole in your chest. Words left unsaid, stuck in your throat. Endings. Deaths. Hospital visits. The sting of antiseptic and beeping machines. Friendships that slipped away. Promises broken over and over again. Love that ran out too fast. Bad phone calls. The offhand words that sink under the skin. Homes that emptied while you were gone. Belonging only partially. Grief that hit without warning and stayed too long. The claw marks on things you would not let go. The blood on your hands. Unanswered prayers. The selves you would bury if you could.

I keep those too, with the same hunger — like an animal holding onto a bone — because they are mine, because there is beauty in them too. Scars and storms earn their own splendor.

It is not the big moments that shape a life. It is watching cartoons in the sofa bed with my siblings, messy hair and mismatched pyjamas, bowls of cereal going soggy. It is a night so full of stars you stopped dead on a dark road and thought — so this is what it is for. It is the moments that felt like nothing at the time that will haunt you the most.

And what is a life if not every fear that made the heart pound, every dream we gripped until it bruised, every soul we loved and buried, every dusty road we walked, every thrill, every song that ran through us like wildfire, every dirt-streaked hand, every scraped knee, every quiet moment that sank into us before we knew it was the whole damn life.

You could take everything from me. My bones are full of faces and roads, of grief and laughter, wounds and prayers, of every story I was ever told and every one I lived. I have got a marrow dense with living.

I am the only audience this life will ever have — the only one who will see it whole. And I do not intend to look away.

Life can throw anything at me. I will take it and make it mine.
