I have spent my life in motion. Not always geographically — sometimes only inwardly — but rarely held by a single place long enough to mistake it for permanence. Childhood stretched wide, porous, itinerant. Nights accumulated in motels, yurts, ryokans — the smell of smoke, dogs yipping into the dark, heaters humming too hot, boots thick with mud. Poland, Normandy, Titirangi, Ushguli, Lofoten, Tofino, Etosha, Skagway — all these places registered less as destinations than as intervals. Travel was never leisure. It was the medium of my life.

For a long time, I waited for places to change me — for geography, movement, the accumulation of experience to produce transformation by proximity alone. As if enough crossings, enough arrivals, would eventually recalibrate something essential. It did not work that way. Movement reshapes what surrounds you, shifts parts of you even, but it does not reach what is underneath. It could displace, expose, clarify — but not hold me long enough for anything lasting to take root.

There is a condition that forms when no country, city, or definition fully contains you. Not exile, not displacement. Partial inclusion everywhere. Under these conditions, time behaves differently — it does not accumulate as continuity but as rupture.

Leaving is always final. Streets remain, but their orientation shifts. Shops close. Fences are painted another colour. Trees are cut down. People age, disappear, or die. Geography stays recognisable while its interior reorganises itself without reference to me. What I return to is never quite what I left.

There is a quiet heaviness in moving through the world without ever feeling received by it — like a stray cat navigating familiar streets without belonging to any of them, recognised but unclaimed. I understood eventually that if home exists at all, it will not be found by moving.

So the psyche adapts. I became observational, attentive, patient. Motion taught me to register change without resistance. Stability depended less on attachment than on perception — on noticing what would not last, and remembering precisely because it would not. Continuity could no longer be assumed. It had to be built from the inside.

In time, I recognised that it was through stories I found stability. The place where the world finally stopped slipping. The quiet force that makes experience matter, that steadies mind and heart when nothing else will.

Stories became a form of apricity — a warmth. They held what would otherwise scatter: the films that shaped my childhood, the tales my grandfather told at dusk, the poems my mother scrawled on walls, the songs I hummed all day long, the family lore, the TV shows that altered my soul — entire worlds that remained endlessly accessible. They provided recurrence where continuity failed. Internal logic where reality fractured. Habitability where no place could be stayed in long enough to settle.

It was never about where I was. It was what I brought into coherence. The mind is never empty — it speaks while holding old phone numbers, obsolete addresses, words heard and never discarded. Stories became a way of holding this excess without letting it scatter. They governed the internal weather. Settled it all into something inhabitable.

They did not distract me from life. They rendered it coherent. Made it meaningful, and therefore worth attention. That reliability was not aesthetic. It was survival.

My internal worlds became a shelter I could carry anywhere. Writing gathers my only true belongings — stories. Geography, memory, mood, desire pass through as material. Neither elevated nor redeemed. Held carefully, through the prism of my own perspective. Not to explain or persuade, but to pass on the flame, and to store what I carry — unearthed remains of the dreamt, the gone, and the road.

It is reliable, as reliable as human recount can be. It obeys internal rules. Preserves enough stability for attention to settle, for thought to continue, for life to remain inhabitable.

It is not a refuge from the world. It is a space constructed within me, where presence can persist. Mist gathers here, lingering when everything else has gone.
